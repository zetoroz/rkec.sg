What the landlord actually checks

Most tenancy agreements ask for the unit to be returned in the condition it was received, fair wear and tear excepted. At the handover inspection that usually comes down to five things:

  • Walls. Screw holes from shelves and TV brackets, Blu Tack marks, scuffs behind the sofa and bed, and hairline cracks. This is where most deposit deductions come from. See paint touch-up.
  • Aircon. Many leases require servicing during the tenancy and a service before handover. Keep the receipt; landlords ask for it.
  • Kitchen. Hood, hob and the grease line on the backsplash tiles, plus inside the cabinets.
  • Bathrooms. Limescale on glass and taps, black silicone, and floor traps.
  • Anything broken. Toilet doors, cabinet hinges, blinds, bulbs.

Why the order of work matters

Doing make-good and cleaning as separate bookings is not only more expensive, it is done in the wrong order more often than not. Patching and sanding a wall puts fine dust over a floor that has just been cleaned, and aircon servicing drips onto it. The order we work in:

  1. Repairs and patching first: holes filled, cracks raked out and filled, anything broken fixed.
  2. Paint touch-up or repaint once the filler is sanded.
  3. Aircon servicing, before the floors are done.
  4. Varnish, if any, given time to dry before anyone walks on it.
  5. Deep clean last, so the unit is at its best on inspection day.

For a two-bedroom unit this is usually one to two days on site. Book it after your furniture has moved out and a few days before the inspection, so there is room to deal with anything the landlord flags.

Post-renovation cleaning

A post-renovation clean is a different job from a move-out clean. The dirt is fine dust rather than grease, and it gets everywhere the works touched: inside new cabinets and drawers, along window and sliding door tracks, in grilles, on top of door frames and in the aircon. There are usually cement and paint spots on the tiles, sticker glue on the glass, and a film of haze on new floors.

  • Dust from the top down: ceilings, light fittings, aircon covers and the tops of cabinets first, so nothing falls on a clean floor.
  • Inside every new cabinet and wardrobe, where sawdust from the carpentry settles.
  • Tracks and grilles: window channels, sliding door tracks and window grilles, which hold the most dust.
  • Residue: cement and grout haze, paint spots and protective film glue removed without scratching new tiles, glass or laminate.
  • Floors last, with a chemical clean on tiled areas to lift the haze.

Post-renovation cleaning is quoted from photos once the works have finished, because how much dust and residue a job leaves varies more than floor area does. If RK E&C does the renovation, the clean is part of the handover. If another contractor did the works, send us photos of each room and we will quote a fixed price. It removes the most common reasons for deductions: marked and holed walls, dirty aircon, a greasy kitchen and black silicone. What your landlord can deduct depends on your tenancy agreement and on fair wear and tear, so read the reinstatement clause before booking and tell us what it says.

How far ahead should I book?

About one to two weeks before your handover. The work should happen after your furniture has moved out and a few days before the inspection, which leaves time to fix anything the landlord flags.

Do you do HDB flats as well as condos?

Do you do post-renovation cleaning?
